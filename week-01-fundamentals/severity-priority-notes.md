# SEVERITY & PRIORITY

## What is the difference between severity and priority

- Severity: The tester confirms how much the system is broken.
- Priority: Confirms how urgent the business needs this fix relative to what is on the team's plate.

- Four bug examples and their correct placement.
  - High severity/high priority: The login button is broken, and the user can't proceed to the next step, which is the dashboard of the loan application. That will make the customer look for another loan app option and will result in the company losing revenue.
  - High Severity/High Priority: The PM is to present the final system to the directors and CEOs, and the loan payment dashboard is not displaying; it is broken.
  - High severity/low priority: The app on an old android version phone is crashing , but according to the company static of user type of phone user usage it is discovered that only a scope of 1% are the old android user, so this doesn't affect the company much compared to the same problem affecting 30% of users.
  - Low priority/low severity: The app's logo cannot be seen clearly, or there is a typo in the company's brand name.

  - Four defense mechanisms I could use when I am defending my call to the PM who disagrees.

  - Know your scope: Know who is affected and how many before the conversation.
  - Use Risk Acceptance writing: If the PM decides to proceed, make sure he or she signs off to communicate to the team that it is his or her decision and not QA's oversight.
  - Know business consequences when the bug is shipped to production: compliance regulations, revenue loss, reputation damage.
  - Be a problem solver rather than being a blocker; look for the possibility of a middle ground, a workaround, or a capped launch.

  - Risk Acceptance Writing: It is a state where if the PM decides to proceed with launching the app, even after reasoning it out, you protect yourself by making the PM responsible for the risk so that the team knows this was a conscious business decision, not a QA oversight.
